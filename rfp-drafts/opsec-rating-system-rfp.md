## Why this matters

A team's keys, devices, multisig process, and access controls are just as important as its contracts. Every serious team invests in operational security, and OPSEC audits already happen, but none of that work is visible: there is no public signal that tells users, investors, or partners who runs a tight ship.

This RFP funds the creation of that signal: a single, industry-agreed OPSEC rating, the way Moody's is the rating investors demand in credit. Teams that earn an A, AA, or AAA appear on a public board with their tier. The board publishes tiers only, never the specific weaknesses behind a score, because a public list of what a team lacks is a target list for attackers. Teams with unacceptable OPSEC simply don't make the board, which says something on its own.

## What this actually pays for

This is a coordination RFP. The recipient is not primarily building software; they are leading a social exercise: getting the community of OPSEC auditing firms to come together and agree on one standard.

- **One scoring template and tier definitions (A / AA / AAA)**, agreed on by at least six OPSEC auditing companies. Roughly: an A-rated team follows best practice and is expected to have no incidents; AAA is security well beyond what's reasonable.
- **The firms already doing OPSEC audits become the raters.** They add one step to their existing flow: apply the agreed scoring template and issue the tier. The first batch of accredited raters is the same group of firms that agreed on the standard.
- **A simple public board** showing rated teams and their tiers. This is a lightweight website; the value is in the standard and the names on it, not the platform.
- **A sustainable structure.** The accredited firms form a membership group — a cooperative professional body of sorts — that maintains the standard, certifies raters, and upholds quality. A small committee (paid on the order of $2,000 per member per cycle) verifies that ratings are sound and standards are upheld, so the rating stays meaningful without anyone profiting from inflating scores.
- **A six-month cycle.** Ratings are reassessed every six months; the scoring template is revisited on the same cadence.

Proposers may modify this solution, but the goal is fixed: a decentralized rating system that many OPSEC auditing companies co-own and agree on, with a credible path to sustaining itself after this grant.

## Who we expect to do this

There is no pre-selected recipient. An open bidding process starts once the RFP is fully funded, and the winning team is selected through the process below. The strongest candidate is a credible coordinator within the security community — someone who can bring six or more OPSEC auditing firms to the same table and get them to sign the same document.

## Scope

**In scope**

- Coordinating at least six OPSEC auditing companies to agree on one scoring template and the A / AA / AAA tier definitions
- Onboarding those firms as the first accredited raters, with the materials they need to apply the template inside their existing audit flow
- A simple public board of rated teams and tiers — tiers only, no details, no gaps
- Designing and standing up the membership body: certification of raters, the verification committee, small stipends, and the funding model that sustains it
- The six-month reassessment cycle, for both ratings and the scoring template itself

**Out of scope**

- Performing the underlying OPSEC audits; the accredited firms do that
- Publishing any team's specific weaknesses, unmet controls, or reasons for a tier
- A heavy platform build; the board is deliberately simple

## Hard requirements

1. **At least six OPSEC auditing companies** formally agree to the scoring template and tier definitions, and commit to issuing ratings with it.
2. **Tiers only.** The public board shows a team's tier and nothing more. No gaps, no unmet controls, no rationale.
3. **Open and versioned standard.** The scoring template and tier definitions are public, open source, and versioned, with changes dated and attributable.
4. **Tool-agnostic.** Any tier is reachable regardless of which tools or frameworks a team uses. No vendor's product is required.
5. **Sustainable, capture-resistant governance.** A membership body of accredited firms maintains the standard, with conflict-of-interest rules and a funding model where nobody profits from inflating scores.
6. **Six-month cycle.** Ratings expire and are reassessed every six months.

## Milestones (draft)

These milestones are a draft. Final milestones and payments are negotiated with the selected team and fixed in the grant agreement. Strengthening this draft is part of a winning proposal.

### A - Agreed standard - $50,000

- [ ] Public, versioned scoring template and A / AA / AAA tier definitions
- [ ] At least six OPSEC auditing companies formally signed on to the standard and committed to rating with it
- [ ] Written governance and sustainability model: the membership body, rater certification, verification committee with small stipends, and the funding mechanism

### B - Board and first ratings - $25,000

- [ ] Public board live, showing rated teams and tiers only
- [ ] Accredited firms onboarded and issuing ratings through their existing audit flow
- [ ] First teams rated end to end to validate the flow

### C - Adoption evidence - $75,000

- [ ] At least [N] teams publicly rated on the board by accredited firms
- [ ] At least one full six-month reassessment completed: existing ratings reset and re-scored, with changes reflected on the board
- [ ] The membership body operating on its own funding model, with the verification committee active
- [ ] Public metrics page: teams rated, tiers awarded, accredited firms participating, reassessments completed

## Milestone review and acceptance

- Criteria with objective public evidence (a live page, a published report, a named party confirming) are accepted on sight.
- Judgment calls are signed off by an independent technical reviewer with no ties to the selected team, agreed between Giveth and the team before work begins and named in the grant agreement.
- The reviewer's fee comes out of the milestone payment, or is pro bono. The winning team coordinates their payment.

## Process

- The proposal window opens once the RFP is fully funded and stays open for 15 days.
- Proposals include: team and track record, technical approach, a milestone plan with per-milestone budget (the draft above, or a stronger version), and full disclosures.
- Giveth selects the team within 7 days of the window closing, weighing credibility, price, and strength of the proposed milestones.
- Milestone deliveries are reviewed within 14 days; payment follows acceptance.
- The first milestone can be paid up to 50% in advance so the team has funding to start. If more funds are needed mid-milestone, the team is expected to reach out to the ecosystem for a stop-gap loan.
- If a milestone stalls, the team gets a 21-day deadline to complete it. If they miss it, unused funds become claimable by the donors who backed the RFP for 30 days; unclaimed funds go to TheDAO Security Fund for other initiatives.
